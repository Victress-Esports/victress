# Victress

# Business Purpose, Creating Shared Value & Strategic Direction

**Turn practice into progress.**

## 1. Why Victress Exists

Victress exists to improve how esports players develop, demonstrate their abilities, and access competitive opportunities.

Across esports, players invest significant time practicing, competing, and improving. However, the infrastructure supporting their development and advancement remains fragmented. Performance information is scattered across match files, statistics, screenshots, tournament records, and individual profiles. Teams and coaches often rely on manual reviews and inconsistent information to evaluate players. Recruiters and collegiate programs can struggle to identify emerging talent and understand a player's competitive history.

This creates a gap between the work players put into improving and their ability to demonstrate that progress to others.

Victress addresses this gap by building connected infrastructure for player development, performance intelligence, and talent discovery.

The platform brings together three core products:

**Scrim Intelligence:** Transforms competitive practice data into structured performance information and development insights.

**Player Resume:** Gives players a structured competitive profile that brings together their performance evidence, history, achievements, roles, and relevant highlights.

**Scout Desk:** Provides teams, coaches, collegiate programs, and recruiters with tools to discover, evaluate, and assess players through more structured information.

Together, these products support a connected development pathway:

**Practice → Performance Evidence → Development → Recognition → Opportunity**

Victress is designed for the broader esports ecosystem: aspiring and established competitive players, amateur teams, coaches, collegiate gaming programs, organizations, and talent evaluators.

Its purpose is not limited to one demographic, game, or competitive level. It is to make player development and talent discovery more structured, accessible, and evidence-informed across esports.

## 2. Creating Shared Value Is the Business Strategy

Victress integrates Creating Shared Value (CSV) into its business strategy.

Creating Shared Value, developed by Michael Porter and Mark Kramer, is the principle that businesses can strengthen their competitiveness while addressing meaningful social and economic challenges. Rather than treating social impact as a separate charitable activity, CSV connects the business's products, operations, and ecosystem relationships to opportunities for shared economic and social progress.

For Victress, this means building a stronger esports development and recruitment ecosystem while intentionally addressing barriers that limit women's participation, development, recognition, and access to competitive opportunities.

This commitment does not change who Victress serves. The commercial platform remains designed for all esports players and organizations. Instead, Victress will use its existing products, business capabilities, and ecosystem relationships to help address a specific challenge within the industry: women competitors often face additional barriers to accessing development resources, demonstrating their abilities, receiving recognition, and progressing through competitive pathways.

Victress will pursue CSV through the three mechanisms identified by Porter and Kramer:

**Reconceiving products and markets:** Developing products and services that address unmet needs and expand access to valuable opportunities.

**Redefining productivity in the value chain:** Improving the way the business operates so that stronger business performance also produces meaningful social benefits.

**Enabling local cluster development:** Strengthening the surrounding ecosystem by working with communities, organizations, institutions, and industry participants to address shared barriers and infrastructure gaps.

These are not three separate charitable programs. They are three connected ways of building a stronger business and a healthier esports ecosystem.

For Victress, the relationship is straightforward:

Better development and recruitment infrastructure creates value for players and organizations.

More accessible and credible performance information can help address barriers to recognition and evaluation.

Stronger community and institutional connections can improve competitive pathways and expand the ecosystem in which Victress operates.

Victress has three commercial products, three integrated CSV dimensions, and one intentional commitment to advancing women in esports as part of its broader business purpose.

## 3. How Victress Creates Shared Value

### A. Reconceiving Products and Markets

Victress's first CSV mechanism is built into the products themselves.

The esports industry has an unmet need for accessible, structured, and useful infrastructure that helps players develop, demonstrate their competitive experience, and become discoverable by teams and organizations.

Victress addresses this need through its three connected products.

**Scrim Intelligence — Making performance information more accessible**

Competitive practice generates valuable information, but much of it is difficult to organize, interpret, and use consistently. Scrim Intelligence is intended to turn practice data into structured performance information that players and teams can use to understand performance and identify areas for development.

Shared value is created by:

- Making performance information more accessible to players and teams.
- Reducing dependence on fragmented records and entirely manual review processes.
- Supporting more informed development conversations.
- Helping players demonstrate their competitive activity through structured evidence.
- Making performance intelligence more accessible to communities and teams that may lack extensive analytical resources.

**Player Resume — Improving recognition and representation**

Players need ways to communicate their competitive experience beyond informal claims, isolated statistics, or scattered achievements.

Player Resume provides a structured way to present a player's competitive identity and supporting information.

Shared value is created by:

- Giving players a consistent place to present their competitive history and achievements.
- Helping players communicate their experience to teams, coaches, and evaluators.
- Making relevant performance information easier to discover and review.
- Supporting recognition for players whose experience may otherwise be difficult to see.
- Helping address visibility gaps that can affect women and other underrepresented competitors.

**Scout Desk — Improving talent discovery and evaluation**

Teams, coaches, collegiate programs, and recruiters need better ways to identify and assess emerging talent.

Scout Desk is intended to make player discovery and evaluation more structured by bringing relevant player information and performance evidence into a connected environment.

Shared value is created by:

- Helping organizations discover players beyond their immediate networks.
- Making relevant competitive information easier to review.
- Supporting more consistent and contextual player evaluation.
- Connecting player development information with recruitment opportunities.
- Expanding the potential talent pool available to teams and collegiate programs.

**How this supports women in esports**

Victress will intentionally examine how these products can address barriers experienced by women competitors.

This includes listening to women players and organizations to understand where existing development, performance documentation, visibility, and recruitment systems fall short. Insights from that engagement will inform product priorities and potential access initiatives.

The objective is not to build a separate platform for women. It is to ensure that the broader infrastructure Victress develops can also address the specific barriers that have limited women's ability to participate, demonstrate their abilities, and progress within esports.

### B. Redefining Productivity in the Value Chain

The second CSV mechanism is improving the way Victress creates and delivers value.

A business's value chain includes the activities involved in developing, operating, delivering, and supporting its products. Victress will integrate shared-value considerations into these activities so that better business practices also contribute to a more trustworthy and accessible esports environment.

Key areas include:

**Data integrity and transparency**

Victress will distinguish between information derived from parsed match data, information obtained through supported integrations, and information submitted by players.

This distinction helps players, teams, and evaluators understand where information comes from and what it can reasonably demonstrate.

The objective is to improve the reliability and interpretability of competitive information, not to suggest that any single data point can establish a player's complete ability or potential.

**Contextual and consistent evaluation**

Victress will work toward presenting player information in ways that support more informed and consistent evaluation.

Performance statistics should be understood in context, including competitive history, role, experience, and available evidence. Structured information can help reduce reliance on incomplete impressions, although it cannot eliminate bias or replace human judgment.

This matters across the player population and is particularly relevant to women competitors who may encounter additional barriers to recognition and fair consideration.

**Privacy, consent, and responsible data practices**

Victress will make responsible handling of player information part of its operating approach.

This includes clear expectations around data collection, player control, consent, appropriate access, and the use of competitive information.

Trustworthy infrastructure benefits every participant and is especially important when building systems intended to support players who may face additional safety, privacy, or representation concerns.

**Accessibility and usability**

Victress will consider the practical barriers that prevent players and organizations from accessing or using performance and recruitment infrastructure.

This includes evaluating usability, affordability, onboarding, and the needs of grassroots teams and developing competitors.

**Responsible platform development**

Victress will incorporate feedback from players, teams, coaches, and organizations into product improvements. It will also evaluate whether its features and processes create unintended barriers to participation or recognition.

**Shared Value Outcome**

These practices strengthen the quality, trustworthiness, and usefulness of Victress's platform while supporting a more accessible and responsible competitive environment.

They are not separate social programs. They are part of building a business that players and organizations can trust.

### C. Enabling Competitive Ecosystem Development

The third CSV mechanism extends beyond Victress's internal operations and products.

Porter and Kramer's concept of cluster development recognizes that businesses depend on the strength of the surrounding ecosystem. Communities, educational institutions, organizations, service providers, industry networks, and other supporting institutions all influence whether an industry can develop and compete effectively.

Victress will apply this principle to esports by engaging directly with the communities and organizations that shape player development and competitive opportunity.

This means identifying shared challenges, listening to the people experiencing them, and collaborating with organizations that are positioned to help address those challenges.

**Community engagement and direct participation**

Victress will seek input from players, grassroots teams, women competitors, community leaders, coaches, and tournament organizers.

The purpose is to understand actual barriers affecting development and progression rather than assume that a platform alone can solve them.

Engagement may include:

- Listening to women competitors about barriers to practice resources, competitive participation, visibility, recognition, and recruitment.
- Consulting grassroots team leaders and community organizers about infrastructure and development needs.
- Identifying gaps in performance documentation, competitive records, and access to evaluation.
- Working with community members to identify practical initiatives that Victress and its partners can realistically support.
- Using community feedback to inform product development and broader ecosystem initiatives.

**Grassroots and women's competitive ecosystem development**

Victress will explore ways to support grassroots competitive communities, including women's teams and organizations, through access to relevant development and performance infrastructure.

Potential areas of collaboration include:

- Helping grassroots teams understand and use structured performance information.
- Exploring access to Scrim Intelligence and Player Resume for qualifying community or development initiatives.
- Supporting better documentation of competitive activity and player achievements.
- Identifying gaps between grassroots participation and higher-level competitive opportunities.
- Working with existing organizations to strengthen development resources rather than duplicate their work.

Any access initiatives or support programs will be developed based on feasibility, community need, and Victress's available resources.

**Collegiate and educational partnerships**

Collegiate esports programs and educational institutions are important parts of the broader competitive ecosystem.

Victress will pursue relationships with these institutions to explore how structured performance information, player profiles, and talent discovery tools can support player development and collegiate recruitment.

Potential areas of collaboration include:

- Exploring how collegiate programs can use structured player information in recruitment and evaluation.
- Improving connections between grassroots competitors and collegiate opportunities.
- Identifying opportunities to support women competitors pursuing collegiate esports pathways.
- Gathering feedback from coaches and program leaders about gaps in player development and evaluation.

**Industry collaboration and competitive pathways**

Victress will also seek to work with teams, leagues, tournament organizers, coaches, recruiters, and other esports organizations.

These relationships can help identify shared infrastructure gaps and opportunities to improve how players move through the competitive ecosystem.

Potential areas of collaboration include:

- Improving the availability and consistency of competitive player information.
- Exploring better ways to document participation, performance, and achievements.
- Connecting grassroots communities with teams, coaches, collegiate programs, and talent evaluators.
- Identifying barriers that prevent emerging competitors from being recognized or considered for opportunities.
- Developing collaborative initiatives that strengthen the broader talent development pipeline.

**Shared Value Outcome**

The objective is to strengthen the ecosystem in which Victress operates.

Players benefit from improved access to development resources and competitive pathways. Teams and institutions benefit from better-connected talent and information. Communities benefit from stronger infrastructure and collaboration. Victress benefits from a healthier, more connected ecosystem in which its products and services can create greater value.

This work must be based on genuine community needs and collaborative solutions. Sponsorships, donations, awareness campaigns, and partnerships alone do not constitute CSV unless they contribute to addressing an underlying social or economic challenge while strengthening the business and its surrounding ecosystem.

## 4. Women in Esports: An Intentional Shared-Value Commitment

Victress serves the broader esports community. Its commitment to women in esports is an intentional application of its Creating Shared Value strategy, not a limitation on its customer base or commercial purpose.

Women participate across competitive esports as players, teammates, coaches, organizers, and leaders. However, women competitors can encounter barriers involving access to competitive environments, development resources, visibility, recognition, representation, and recruitment.

These challenges are not solved simply by encouraging more women to participate. They also require stronger infrastructure, more accessible development opportunities, better ways to demonstrate competitive experience, and stronger connections between communities and organizations.

Victress believes its existing business capabilities can contribute to addressing these challenges.

The commitment is to use the broader Victress platform and ecosystem relationships to help improve:

**Access:** Make relevant performance and development infrastructure more accessible to women competitors and grassroots teams.

**Development:** Support the ability to review, understand, and document competitive performance.

**Visibility:** Help women competitors present their competitive experience through structured player profiles and supporting information.

**Recognition:** Make relevant competitive achievements and performance evidence easier for teams and organizations to discover.

**Evaluation:** Support more consistent, contextual, evidence-informed consideration of players.

**Opportunity:** Strengthen connections between grassroots communities, collegiate programs, teams, coaches, and recruiters.

**Community engagement and direct participation**

Victress will not assume that it already understands every barrier women competitors face.

It will seek direct input from women players, women's esports communities, grassroots organizations, collegiate programs, and other relevant stakeholders.

That engagement will help determine:

- Which barriers are most significant in different competitive environments.
- Where existing infrastructure is insufficient or inaccessible.
- Which product improvements would provide practical value.
- Where collaboration with existing organizations is more effective than creating something independently.
- Which initiatives can produce measurable improvements in development access, visibility, recognition, and competitive pathways.

Community feedback will inform product priorities, operational practices, and potential collaborative initiatives.

**A commitment within the broader business**

Women in Esports is not a fourth product, a separate commercial platform, or a replacement for Victress's core business.

It is an intentional shared-value focus that informs how Victress develops its products, operates its business, and engages with the esports ecosystem.

The resulting infrastructure should remain useful to the broader player population. Improvements in performance documentation, data transparency, accessibility, evaluation, and recruitment can benefit all competitors while helping address barriers that disproportionately affect women.

## 5. Business Model

Victress's commercial business remains centered on its connected player development and recruitment platform.

Its three products work together to support the player journey from practice to opportunity.

| Product | Primary Function | Value Created |
|---|---|---|
| Scrim Intelligence | Performance analysis and development insights | Helps players and teams understand competitive practice |
| Player Resume | Structured competitive profile and player representation | Helps players communicate their experience and achievements |
| Scout Desk | Player discovery and evaluation | Helps organizations identify and assess talent |

**Revenue Model**

Victress uses a subscription-based model with offerings designed for different levels of participation and organizational needs.

| Plan | Intended User | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Rookie | Individual players beginning with the platform | Free |
| Pro | Individual players seeking expanded platform capabilities | $7.99/month or $75/year |
| Team | Competitive teams and organizations | $49/month |
| Scout | Recruiters and talent evaluators | $249/month |

These offerings support a commercial model in which individual players can begin using the platform, competitive teams can access tools relevant to their development, and recruiters and organizations can use dedicated discovery capabilities.

**How CSV strengthens the business model**

Creating Shared Value is integrated into this model rather than operating as a separate revenue stream.

Better and more accessible player development infrastructure creates value for individual players.

More structured competitive information creates value for teams and coaches.

Better-connected player profiles and discovery capabilities create value for recruiters and collegiate programs.

Responsible data practices and contextual evaluation strengthen platform trust.

Community engagement and ecosystem development can reveal unmet needs, inform product improvements, and expand the network of players and organizations that benefit from Victress.

The commercial business supports the long-term capacity to create shared value. At the same time, addressing real ecosystem challenges can strengthen the relevance and usefulness of the commercial platform.

Victress will pursue social impact in ways that are operationally realistic and financially sustainable. It will not promise programs, access, or services that it cannot reliably deliver.

## 6. Build and Measure Impact

Victress will measure shared value through both business performance and meaningful improvements in the esports ecosystem.

Impact measurement must go beyond counting partnerships, events, participants, or promotional activity. The goal is to understand whether Victress's products and collaborative initiatives are contributing to actual improvements.

Measurement will develop alongside the platform and the initiatives Victress is able to implement.

**Business and platform measures**

Potential measures include:

- Player registrations and active platform usage.
- Adoption of Scrim Intelligence, Player Resume, and Scout Desk.
- Individual and organizational subscriptions.
- Player profile completion and use.
- Team and recruiter engagement.
- Platform retention and customer satisfaction.
- Product adoption and usage across different competitive communities.

**Player development and access measures**

Potential measures include:

- Players and teams using performance analysis tools.
- Frequency of performance documentation and review.
- Improvements in access to structured development resources.
- Grassroots teams participating in relevant access initiatives.
- Player feedback on the usefulness of performance information.
- Evidence that players can more effectively communicate their competitive experience.

**Women in esports measures**

Victress will evaluate the impact of its intentional women-in-esports commitment through measures such as:

- Women competitors and organizations engaged through direct consultation.
- Recurring participation from women players and community representatives in feedback.
- Women-led or women's grassroots teams accessing relevant development infrastructure through implemented initiatives.
- Use of player profiles and performance tools by women competitors.
- Collaborative initiatives addressing barriers identified by women competitors.
- Improvements in visibility, performance documentation, and access to relevant competitive networks.
- Connections facilitated between grassroots communities, collegiate programs, teams, coaches, and recruiters.
- Feedback from participants on whether initiatives address the challenges they identified.

**Competitive ecosystem measures**

Potential measures include:

- Relationships developed with grassroots, collegiate, and competitive organizations.
- Collaborative projects launched and completed.
- Identified ecosystem gaps addressed through practical initiatives.
- Connections established between players and relevant organizations.
- Improvements in access to development and evaluation infrastructure.
- Evidence of stronger coordination between participating communities and institutions.

**Value-chain and trust measures**

Potential measures include:

- Clear data provenance and transparency practices.
- Implementation of privacy, consent, and access controls.
- Player understanding and confidence in how their information is used.
- Improvements to platform accessibility and usability.
- Feedback on the clarity and contextual usefulness of player information.

**Measurement principles**

Victress will distinguish between activity, output, and outcome.

For example, holding a community meeting is an activity. Developing a collaborative performance-resource initiative is an output. Demonstrating that participating teams gained meaningful access to development infrastructure is an outcome.

Similarly, creating a player profile is not proof that a player received an opportunity. Victress will distinguish between making players more discoverable and demonstrating that a team, program, or recruiter acted on that information.

Where possible, Victress will establish baselines, track progress over time, and use participant feedback to assess whether initiatives are producing meaningful results.

## 7. Long-Term Strategic Direction

Victress's long-term direction is to become connected infrastructure for esports player development, performance intelligence, and talent discovery.

The business will grow by strengthening the relationship between its three core products and expanding the network of players and organizations that use them.

**Platform Development**

Victress will continue developing:

- Scrim Intelligence as a foundation for structured practice analysis and performance understanding.
- Player Resume as a consistent way to document and communicate competitive experience.
- Scout Desk as a resource for structured player discovery and evaluation.

Development will prioritize reliable functionality, useful player information, and a connected experience across the platform.

**Ecosystem Development**

Victress will pursue relationships with grassroots teams, collegiate programs, competitive organizations, tournament organizers, coaches, recruiters, and relevant esports communities.

These relationships will help identify shared challenges, improve the usefulness of the platform, and strengthen connections between different levels of competitive participation.

**Shared-Value Development**

Victress will continue identifying practical opportunities to address barriers within esports, with an intentional focus on women competitors.

This work will evolve through community input, product development, collaborative initiatives, and measurable outcomes.

The goal is to demonstrate how a commercial esports platform can contribute to improving the industry while building a stronger and more sustainable business.

**Sustainable Growth**

Victress will expand based on product readiness, demonstrated demand, operational capacity, and evidence of impact.

It will prioritize building a reliable business and meaningful ecosystem relationships rather than pursuing expansion or social-impact commitments that cannot be sustained.

## 8. Victress Commitment

Victress is committed to building better infrastructure for esports player development and recruitment.

It will serve the broader esports ecosystem while intentionally using its business capabilities to help address barriers that affect women competitors.

This commitment will be reflected in actions:

- Developing products that make performance information, player representation, and talent discovery more accessible.
- Building responsible data practices that support trust, transparency, privacy, and informed evaluation.
- Engaging directly with communities to understand real barriers and identify practical solutions.
- Working with grassroots organizations, collegiate programs, teams, and industry participants to strengthen competitive pathways.
- Measuring business performance and social outcomes together.
- Growing initiatives based on demonstrated need, meaningful results, and the ability to sustain them.

Victress does not view social impact as something separate from building a successful business. It sees an opportunity to strengthen the esports ecosystem by addressing challenges that affect how players develop, demonstrate their abilities, and access opportunities.

The business serves esports. The shared-value strategy helps make esports more accessible, connected, and opportunity-driven—including for women who have historically faced additional barriers to advancement.
